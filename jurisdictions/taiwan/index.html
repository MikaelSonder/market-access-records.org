Introduction

Medical device registration in Taiwan is the structured regulatory and operational function through which a business determines whether a product is regulated as a medical device, identifies the applicable Taiwan risk class and secures the correct product-license, quality-system and business-permit pathway required for lawful commercialization.

In practical terms, Taiwan combines TFDA risk-based classification, product registration, QSD or QMS conformity assessment, local representative structure and medical device business permits in one integrated market-entry framework.

The subject is commercially important because Taiwan is a mature medtech market, while mistakes around class, local applicant structure, QSD readiness, business-permit scope, labeling or import authorization can delay or block lawful market entry.

Executive Summary

Medical device registration in Taiwan is the professional regulatory and market-access function concerned with determining whether a product falls within the Taiwan medical-device framework, identifying its risk class and securing the correct TFDA product-license and quality-system route for lawful commercialization.

Taiwan uses a risk-based system with Class I, Class II and Class III devices. Public TFDA-facing materials and industry guidance aligned to TFDA practice explain that products are classified into three risk levels and that pre-market approval is required before entry into the Taiwan market.

The pathway is therefore class-driven, but it is also structure-driven. TFDA’s official business-permit instruction states that for medical device registration, a copy of the medical device business permit is needed, and that importers must attach a dealer permit showing that the business category includes medical device import.

For foreign market-entry planning, quality-system conformity and local representation are central. TFDA’s official QMS and QSD page provides application routes for QSD conformity assessment for foreign manufacturers of imported medical devices, while Taiwan registration guidance explains that a foreign manufacturer must appoint a Taiwan agent or local representative to apply for registration.

Definition

Medical device registration in Taiwan refers to the structured process through which a business determines whether its product is regulated as a medical device, identifies the relevant class and secures the appropriate product-license, quality-system and import-structure position before commercialization.

The object is broader than filing a product application. It covers device qualification, class mapping, local representative structure, QSD or QMS conformity assessment, product dossier preparation, labeling in Traditional Chinese, business-permit scope, import authorization and post-license maintenance.

Scope

The Taiwan medical device registration function applies to products regulated as medical devices under the Medical Device Act framework and to the companies responsible for lawfully importing, distributing or manufacturing those devices in Taiwan.

The scope is both product-facing and structure-facing. TFDA’s official materials show that product registration, QSD conformity and medical device business permits are interlinked, because registration requires the appropriate business permit and importers must hold a dealer permit that specifically includes medical device import.

Editorial Note: In Taiwan, a registration review is incomplete if it looks only at the product dossier and ignores whether the applicant and importer structure has the correct business permit and QSD pathway.

Purpose

The purpose of medical device registration in Taiwan is to convert a product, quality file and commercialization plan into a lawful TFDA market-entry position.

In business terms, the function exists to identify the correct class-based route, determine whether QSD conformity applies, align the Taiwan local representative and importer structure and reduce the risk of entering Taiwan on an unsupported regulatory assumption.

Primary Outcome

A coherent Taiwan medical device registration position means that the device has been qualified correctly, matched to the applicable Class I, II or III category and linked to the correct TFDA product-license route and local import structure for lawful commercialization.

Depending on class and product type, the outcome may include a QSD conformity assessment for the foreign manufacturing site, a TFDA product license and import-authorization support for authorized local distributors. Public Taiwan guidance explains that license holders or authorized companies must submit an authorization application to TFDA to import the product.

Jurisdiction Characteristics

Taiwan is a TFDA-centered medical-device jurisdiction with clear risk classes, structured quality-system review and strong dependence on local permit architecture for imported products.

A distinctive practical feature is the split between quality-system and product-licensing work. TFDA’s official site provides separate application routes for QSD conformity assessment for foreign manufacturers of imported medical devices, while TFDA’s business-permit instructions make clear that the correct permit copy must accompany registration and import-related filings.

Operational CultureFormal, document-driven and strongly structured around quality-system evidence and local permit status.

Process Flow

Taiwan medical device registration normally works as a staged process from product qualification to commercialization. Public Taiwan guidance aligned with TFDA practice describes two main phases: site or quality-system registration and product registration.

1. Product QualificationDetermine whether the product is regulated as a medical device in Taiwan.
2. Risk ClassificationIdentify the applicable Class I, II or III category.
3. Local StructureAppoint or confirm the Taiwan agent, local representative or importer structure that will hold the application or import role.
4. Business-Permit ReviewConfirm that the relevant local entity holds the correct medical device business permit and import-capable dealer category if applicable.
5. QSD or QMS Conformity AssessmentPrepare and submit the quality-system conformity application for the manufacturing site where required.
6. Technical DocumentationPrepare the product dossier, labeling, authorizations, certificates and test evidence needed for the product-license filing.
7. Product RegistrationSubmit the registration application to TFDA for product licensing.
8. Import Authorization StructureArrange any required authorization filings so that the approved product can be imported by authorized parties.
9. Market LaunchCommercialize the device after the correct quality, product and permit positions are in place.
10. Post-Market MaintenanceMaintain renewals, labeling updates, authorization changes and continuing compliance after launch.
Typical OutputsQualification memo, class rationale, QSD file, local-representative file, business-permit file, product-license dossier, import-authorization file and maintenance file.

Decision Tree

The decision tree helps simplify the main Taiwan threshold questions.

  1. Confirm whether the product is regulated as a medical device in Taiwan.
  2. Determine the risk class because Taiwan uses Class I, II and III.
  3. Establish which Taiwan entity will act as local representative, agent or importer.
  4. Confirm whether the relevant Taiwan entity holds the correct medical device business permit and, for importers, a dealer permit that includes medical device import.
  5. Determine whether QSD conformity assessment is required for the manufacturing site.
  6. Prepare the product dossier, Free Sale Certificate, authorization documents, labeling and technical evidence.
  7. Confirm how import authorization will work for the license holder or authorized companies.
  8. Commercialize only after the product-license and local permit architecture are both secured.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is high because many non-Taiwan manufacturers rely on Taiwan agents, importers and business-permit structures to enter the market. Public Taiwan guidance explains that a foreign manufacturer must appoint a Taiwan agent or local representative, and that only certificate holders or authorized companies can import the product.

The cross-border issue is therefore not simply whether the product is approved elsewhere. It is whether the manufacturer has mapped the Taiwan class, QSD obligations, local representative structure, business-permit scope, Traditional Chinese labeling and import-authorization logic required by TFDA practice.
