Introduction

Medical device registration in Vietnam is the structured regulatory and operational function through which a business determines whether a product is regulated as a medical device, identifies the applicable Vietnamese risk class and secures the correct declaration or product-registration route required for lawful import and commercialization.

In practical terms, Vietnam combines Ministry of Health oversight, Decree 98, provincial Department of Health declarations for lower-risk devices, Ministry-level registration for higher-risk devices and portal-based submissions through the national medical-device system.

The subject is commercially important because Vietnam is a large imported-device market, while mistakes around classification, local responsible organization, declaration route, registration-number requirements or import conditions can delay or block lawful market entry.

Executive Summary

Medical device registration in Vietnam is the professional regulatory and market-access function concerned with determining whether a product falls within the Vietnamese medical-device framework, identifying its risk class and securing the correct authority pathway for lawful commercialization.

The framework is now anchored in Decree 98. The official U.S. Department of Commerce Vietnam registration overview states that Decree No. 98/2021/ND-CP took effect on 1 January 2022 and replaced the prior medical-device decrees that had governed registration and import structures.

The route is class-driven and authority-driven. The same official overview states that, under Decree 98, the local Department of Health is responsible for notification of Class A and B medical devices, while Class C and D medical devices are subject to product registration with the Ministry of Health.

The filing logic is therefore split between declaration of applied standards for lower-risk products and product registration for higher-risk products, with online filing through the national portal and post-market inspection used as part of the compliance model.

Definition

Medical device registration in Vietnam refers to the structured process through which a business determines whether its product is regulated as a medical device, identifies the relevant Vietnamese class and secures the appropriate declaration or product-registration route before commercialization.

The object is broader than filing a portal application. It covers product qualification, A to D classification, local responsible-organization structuring, declaration strategy for Class A and B, product registration for Class C and D, dossier preparation, import-position analysis, labeling and post-market maintenance.

Scope

The Vietnam medical device registration function applies to products regulated as medical devices and to the organizations responsible for placing those products on the Vietnamese market.

The scope is both product-facing and structure-facing. The official Vietnam registration overview states that medical devices are classified as Type A, B, C or D and that there are mainly two pathways: one for Type A and B, and another for Type C and D, each filed through the national medical-device portal.

Editorial Note: In Vietnam, a registration review is incomplete if it looks only at the product and ignores the local responsible organization, the applicable class route and the long-term validity or maintenance logic of the resulting number.

Purpose

The purpose of medical device registration in Vietnam is to convert a product, local commercialization structure and regulatory dossier into a lawful Ministry of Health market-entry position.

In business terms, the function exists to identify the correct class and authority route, align the local responsible organization, prepare the declaration or registration dossier and reduce the risk of entering Vietnam on an unsupported regulatory assumption.

Primary Outcome

A coherent Vietnam medical device registration position means that the device has been qualified correctly, matched to the applicable Class A, B, C or D category and linked to the correct local authority route for lawful commercialization.

Depending on class, the outcome may be a declaration number for Class A or B, or a registration number for Class C or D. The official Vietnam registration overview states that the Department of Health publishes declaration numbers for Class A and B on the portal, while the Ministry of Health processes Class C and D registration applications and issues the relevant product-registration result.

Jurisdiction Characteristics

Vietnam is a Ministry of Health-centered medical-device jurisdiction with a formal A to D class-based system and a split between local declaration and central registration pathways.

A distinctive practical feature is the authority split. The official Vietnam registration overview states that the local Department of Health handles Class A and B declarations, while the Ministry of Health handles Class C and D product registration, all through the national medical-device portal.

Process Flow

Vietnam medical device registration normally works as a staged process from product qualification to commercialization. The official Vietnam registration overview states that both lower-risk and higher-risk routes run through the national portal and that post-market inspection is used to control the quality of medical devices imported and circulated in Vietnam.

1. Product QualificationDetermine whether the product is regulated as a medical device in Vietnam.
2. Risk ClassificationIdentify the applicable Class A, B, C or D category.
3. Local Structure MappingIdentify the organization responsible for placing the device on the Vietnamese market and supporting filings.
4. Route DeterminationConfirm whether the route is declaration of applied standards for Class A and B or product registration for Class C and D.
5. Dossier PreparationPrepare the classification file, authorization materials, technical summary, quality documentation, labels and other required documents.
6. Portal FilingSubmit the application through the national medical-device portal.
7. Authority ReviewReceive publication of declaration number or central authority review, and respond to modification requests where necessary.
8. Registration OutcomeObtain the declaration or registration result that supports lawful circulation.
9. Import and Market PlacementCommercialize only after the correct route outcome and import position are in place.
10. Post-Market MaintenanceMaintain ongoing compliance under post-market inspection and update obligations.
Typical OutputsQualification memo, class rationale, local authorization file, declaration or registration dossier, portal submission record, declaration number or registration number and maintenance file.

Decision Tree

The decision tree helps simplify the main Vietnam threshold questions.

  1. Confirm whether the product is regulated as a medical device in Vietnam.
  2. Determine the risk class because Vietnam uses A, B, C and D categories.
  3. Identify the Vietnamese organization responsible for placing the product on the market.
  4. Assess whether the route is Class A-B declaration or Class C-D product registration.
  5. Prepare the dossier and portal filing package in the correct form.
  6. Respond to any authority modification requests within the permitted timelines.
  7. Secure the relevant declaration or registration outcome before commercialization.
  8. Confirm that import and market-circulation conditions are aligned with the approved route.

Timeline

The Vietnam timeline begins before launch and continues after commercialization. The official Vietnam registration overview states that, for Class C and D devices, the Ministry of Health processes satisfactory applications within 30 days and may request modifications within 25 days of receipt where the dossier is not satisfactory, while applicants have 90 days to respond to modification requests.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is high because Vietnam is heavily import-dependent and foreign manufacturers depend on local filing structures and the correct declaration or registration route. The official Vietnam registration overview states that over 90 percent of medical equipment and supplies are imported into the country.

The cross-border issue is therefore not simply whether the product has prior approvals elsewhere. It is whether the manufacturer has mapped the Vietnam class, the local responsible organization, the correct portal route and the import-position logic required for actual commercialization.
