Introduction

Medical device registration in Australia is the structured regulatory and operational function through which a business determines whether a product is regulated as a medical device, identifies the applicable class and secures lawful market access through the Therapeutic Goods Administration framework.

In practical terms, the Australian route revolves around inclusion in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods, sponsor-led market access and continuing compliance obligations after supply begins.

The subject is commercially important because a device must generally reach the correct TGA position before it can be legally supplied in Australia, and mistakes around classification, sponsor structure or ARTG inclusion can block market entry and create enforcement risk.

Executive Summary

Medical device registration in Australia is the professional regulatory and market-access function concerned with determining whether a product qualifies as a medical device under the Australian framework, identifying its class and achieving the legal position required for supply through the TGA system.

The TGA states that a medical device can be any product or software, including AI, that works to achieve a therapeutic purpose in or on human beings. The same TGA framework explains that medical-device regulation in Australia spans manufacturing, application and market authorisation, supply and distribution, labelling and advertising, and monitoring and compliance. [page:1]

Australian market access is closely connected to the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods. The TGA states that the ARTG is the public database of therapeutic goods that can be legally supplied in Australia. [web:98][page:2]

Registration analysis in Australia is therefore not just a filing exercise. It requires a business to determine whether the product is a therapeutic good, whether it needs to be included in the ARTG and whether the sponsor-led supply structure is set up correctly for lawful supply. [web:92][page:1][page:2]

Definition

Medical device registration in Australia refers to the structured process through which a business determines whether its product is regulated as a medical device or IVD medical device, identifies the relevant Australian class or inclusion logic and secures the TGA and ARTG position needed for lawful supply.

The object is broader than searching the register. It covers product qualification, therapeutic-good analysis, class determination, sponsor structure, ARTG inclusion logic, documentation readiness and continuing post-market responsibilities.

Covered MattersTherapeutic-good qualification, medical-device assessment, class analysis, ARTG inclusion, sponsor structure, market authorisation readiness and ongoing supply compliance.
Functional BoundaryThe Registry Object covers how businesses achieve and maintain lawful Australian market access for medical devices.
Related but Not PrimaryHospital procurement, private reimbursement, state purchasing rules and general distributor contracting are adjacent but not the primary object here.
Outside ScopePharmaceutical authorisation, unrelated therapeutic goods categories, company incorporation and generic commercial strategy unrelated to device lawfulness.

Scope

The Australian medical device registration function applies to products regulated by the TGA as medical devices or IVD medical devices and to the market participants responsible for lawful supply in Australia. The TGA states that the medical-device journey includes application and market authorisation, supply and distribution, labelling and advertising, and monitoring and compliance. [page:1]

The scope question starts with product identity. TGA guidance on whether a product needs to be in the ARTG instructs businesses to determine whether the product is a therapeutic good and whether it is a medical device that needs ARTG inclusion. [web:92]

Editorial Note: In Australia, a registration review is incomplete if it does not first answer two threshold questions: is the product a therapeutic good, and if so, does it need to be included in the ARTG before supply? [web:92][page:2]

Purpose

The purpose of medical device registration in Australia is to convert a product, evidence base and supply model into a lawful TGA market-entry position.

In business terms, the function exists to identify the correct class-based route, confirm whether ARTG inclusion is required, align the sponsor structure and reduce the risk of supplying a device in Australia without the correct legal basis.

Primary Outcome

A coherent Australian medical device registration position means that the product has been qualified correctly, matched to the relevant class-based route and linked to the sponsor and ARTG architecture required for lawful supply.

For devices that must be included in the ARTG, the practical outcome is a register position that can be checked in the public ARTG database, which the TGA describes as the public database of therapeutic goods that can be legally supplied in Australia. [web:98][page:2]

Process Flow

Australian medical device registration normally works as a staged process from product qualification to lawful supply. The TGA’s published framework for medical devices starts with understanding what kind of medical device the product is and continues through application and market authorisation, supply and distribution, and monitoring and compliance. [page:1]

1. Product QualificationDetermine whether the product is a therapeutic good and a medical device in the Australian framework. [web:92][page:1]
2. Class DeterminationIdentify the relevant class, since TGA classification is linked to risk. [web:95]
3. Exemption or Inclusion ReviewConfirm whether the product is exempt from ARTG inclusion or whether inclusion is required before supply. TGA guidance lists certain categories exempt from inclusion, such as some clinical-trial, special-access, personal-importation and custom-made contexts. [web:92]
4. Sponsor StructureEstablish the sponsor-manufacturer relationship required to support the inclusion process and ongoing information access. [web:89]
5. Documentation ReadinessPrepare the regulatory, technical, clinical and safety documentation that the TGA may request. [web:89]
6. ARTG Application or Inclusion StepApply for inclusion where the device must be included in the ARTG before supply. [page:1][page:2]
7. Register VerificationConfirm the ARTG position and search details in the public register. [web:85][page:2]
8. Commercial SupplySupply the device in Australia only after the lawful market-access position is in place.
9. Ongoing ComplianceMaintain obligations relating to supply, distribution, labelling, advertising and post-market monitoring. [page:1]
Typical OutputsQualification memo, class rationale, sponsor file, ARTG inclusion record and ongoing compliance controls.

Decision Tree

The decision tree helps simplify the main Australian threshold questions.

  1. Confirm whether the product is a therapeutic good and whether it is a medical device in the Australian framework. [web:92][page:1]
  2. Determine the device class because the TGA links classification to risk. [web:95]
  3. Check whether the product is excluded from being a medical device or exempt from ARTG inclusion in the specific supply context. [web:92]

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is high because many non-Australian manufacturers enter the market through an Australian sponsor structure. TGA guidance on the inclusion process states that before applying to include a device in the ARTG, a relationship with the manufacturer must be established so the sponsor can obtain documentation and be kept informed of problems, safety alerts or recalls. [web:89]

The cross-border issue in Australia is therefore not simply whether the device exists elsewhere. It is whether the product has the correct Australian qualification, inclusion logic and sponsor-led market-access structure for lawful supply. [web:92][page:2]

RecognitionAustralian market access depends on TGA and ARTG status rather than on automatic recognition of another market’s device authorization.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the main failure points in Australian medical device registration. Many arise when businesses treat the ARTG as a simple listing outcome rather than the result of a correct qualification and sponsor-led market-access strategy.

Qualification RiskThe product is misread as a medical device or non-device, which distorts the Australian route from the start. [web:92][page:1]
Class RiskThe wrong class is assumed, which changes the regulatory pathway. [web:95]
Inclusion RiskThe business assumes the device can be supplied without first determining whether ARTG inclusion is legally required. [web:92][page:2]
Sponsor RiskThe sponsor relationship is not structured well enough to support documentation access, recalls or safety-alert communication. [web:89]

Post-Market RiskThe business neglects supply, distribution, advertising or monitoring responsibilities after launch. [page:1]

FAQ

The FAQ section addresses recurring threshold questions in Australian device market-entry work.

What Is the ARTG?The TGA states that the ARTG is the public database of therapeutic goods that can be legally supplied in Australia. [web:98][page:2]
Does Every Product Need to Be in the ARTG?No. TGA guidance explains that some products may be exempt from inclusion in the ARTG in specific contexts, such as some clinical-trial, special-access, personal-importation and custom-made situations. [web:92]
How Does Australia Define a Medical Device?The TGA states that a medical device can be any product or software, including AI, that works to achieve a therapeutic purpose in or on human beings. [web:84][page:1]
Why Is the Sponsor Important?TGA guidance on the inclusion process explains that the sponsor must have a relationship with the manufacturer that allows access to documentation and ongoing problem or recall information. [web:89]
Can Overseas Approval Alone Authorise Supply in Australia?No. The key issue is whether the product has the correct Australian legal basis, including ARTG status where required. [web:92][page:2]
Does Registration End After Launch?No. The TGA framework includes ongoing supply, distribution, advertising and monitoring obligations after market entry. [page:1]
