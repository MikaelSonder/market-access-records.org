Introduction

Medical device registration in Peru is the structured regulatory and operational function through which a business determines whether a product can be lawfully registered, imported, marketed and distributed under the Peruvian medical-device framework.

In practical terms, Peru combines DIGEMID oversight, a local sanitary-registration holder structure, VUCE-based submission mechanics, a four-class risk model, technical-dossier review, reference-market evidence and post-market duties in one integrated market-access framework.

The subject is commercially important because Peru requires a local holder for foreign manufacturers and uses a class-sensitive dossier model, so mistakes around holder choice, risk classification, submission routing, document sufficiency or renewal timing can materially delay or block commercialization.

Executive Summary

Medical device registration in Peru is the professional regulatory and market-access function concerned with determining whether a product falls within the Peruvian medical-device framework, identifying the correct risk-based route and securing the sanitary registration and importation position required for lawful commercialization.

The route is registration-driven as well as holder-driven. Public Peru guidance states that DIGEMID oversees medical-device regulation, that foreign manufacturers must appoint a local holder or local representative to submit the registration and that applications are commonly processed through the VUCE system.

The framework is also class-sensitive and evidence-driven. Public Peru guidance explains that Peru applies a Class I, II, III and IV risk model, that dossier expectations increase by class and that common supporting materials include technical documentation, QMS evidence, labeling, IFU and either reference-market authorization evidence or a CFS or CFG from the home country.

The practical result is that Peruvian market entry depends on more than a technical dossier alone. A compliant strategy also has to address holder control, class selection, VUCE filing readiness, evidence depth, labeling, post-market obligations and renewal timing after approval.

Definition

Medical device registration in Peru refers to the structured process through which a business determines whether its product is regulated as a medical device, identifies the relevant Peruvian class and secures the appropriate local filing and commercialization route before market entry.

The object is broader than submitting one application. It covers product qualification, class determination, Peru Registration Holder appointment, certificate review, technical-dossier preparation, VUCE routing, labeling review and lifecycle maintenance.

Scope

The Peru medical device registration function applies to products regulated as medical devices and to the local entities responsible for registration, importation and commercialization in Peru.

The scope is both product-facing and holder-facing. Public Peru guidance states that foreign manufacturers without local presence must submit through an Authorized Local Representative or Peru Registration Holder and that the registration is issued to the Peruvian company acting in that role.

Editorial Note: In Peru, a registration review is incomplete if it looks only at the technical dossier and ignores who holds the sanitary registration, who controls local filings and how renewal and import execution will work through the Peruvian holder.

Purpose

The purpose of medical device registration in Peru is to convert a product, local holder structure and regulatory dossier into a lawful DIGEMID market-entry and sanitary-registration position.

In business terms, the function exists to identify the correct class, prepare the file, secure submission acceptance, align the Peru holder structure and reduce the risk of entering Peru on an unsupported regulatory assumption.

Primary Outcome

A coherent Peru medical device registration position means that the device has been qualified correctly, linked to the appropriate Peru Registration Holder and placed in a regulatory position that supports lawful importation and commercialization.

The outcome is broader than a certificate alone. The practical approval architecture also depends on whether the local holder, submission routing and maintenance structure are ready to sustain the registration through renewals, changes and post-market obligations.

Jurisdiction Characteristics

Peru is a DIGEMID-centered medical-device jurisdiction with a registration model that combines risk-based review, local holder control and increasingly digital filing mechanics.

A distinctive practical feature is the interaction between risk class and dossier depth. Public Peru guidance explains that Class I through IV devices are reviewed under a graduated evidence model, that higher-risk devices require more extensive technical and clinical support and that filings are handled through VUCE-linked procedures.

Operational CultureFormal, class-driven and highly sensitive to documentation quality, holder control and procedural completeness.

Process Flow

Peru medical device registration normally works as a staged process from product qualification to commercialization. Public Peru guidance explains that the route starts with classification and local-holder planning, proceeds through dossier compilation and VUCE submission and then continues into DIGEMID review, approval and maintenance.

1. Product QualificationDetermine whether the product is regulated as a medical device in Peru.
2. Classification ReviewIdentify whether the device is Class I, II, III or IV.
3. PRH AppointmentIdentify and appoint the Peru Registration Holder or Authorized Local Representative.
4. Document PreparationCompile product information, technical evidence, QMS documents, labeling, IFU and reference-market or free-sale evidence.
5. VUCE SetupVerify procedures through VUCE, authenticate with local credentials and prepare the relevant SUCE request.
6. SubmissionSubmit the class-appropriate dossier to DIGEMID through the approved route.
7. Review and QueriesUndergo DIGEMID review and respond to requests for clarification or supplementary evidence.
8. ApprovalObtain the sanitary registration certificate needed for commercialization.
9. CommercializationCommercialize through the lawful holder and import structure in Peru.
10. MaintenanceManage renewal, changes, vigilance or tecnovigilance and lifecycle obligations.
Typical OutputsQualification memo, class rationale, PRH appointment file, technical dossier, VUCE submission record, sanitary registration certificate and lifecycle control file.

Decision Tree

The decision tree helps simplify the main Peru threshold questions.

  1. Confirm whether the product is regulated as a medical device in Peru.
  2. Determine whether the device is Class I, II, III or IV.
  3. Identify the Peruvian registration holder or local representative.
  4. Prepare the CFS or CFG, QMS evidence, technical file, labeling and class-appropriate clinical or risk materials.
  5. Verify the procedure in VUCE and prepare the SUCE submission path.
  6. Submit to DIGEMID.
  7. Respond to review questions and obtain approval.
  8. Commercialize through the Peruvian holder that controls the registration and local market-entry execution.

Timeline

The Peru timeline begins before launch and varies by risk class. Public Peru guidance states that Class I review may take around 60 days, Class II around 90 days and Class III and IV around 120 days, while other market summaries describe a practical approval window of roughly 5 to 8 months and state that registrations are valid for 5 years.

Cross-Border Relevance

Cross-border relevance is high because foreign manufacturers generally cannot commercialize in Peru without a local holder and a DIGEMID registration strategy. Public Peru guidance states that foreign manufacturers must appoint a Peru Registration Holder or Authorized Local Representative and that the registration is issued to the Peruvian company.

The cross-border issue is therefore not simply whether the device is marketed elsewhere. It is whether the manufacturer has mapped the Peruvian class route, holder design, evidence package, VUCE process and renewal structure in a commercially workable way.

Operating Constraints & Risks

Operating constraints identify the recurring failure points in Peru medical device registration. Many arise when businesses underestimate how closely holder control, dossier completeness, class selection, VUCE mechanics and renewal duties are linked.

Qualification RiskThe product is misread as a medical device or non-device under Peruvian rules.
Class RiskThe wrong DIGEMID class is assumed, leading to incorrect evidence depth or timing expectations.
Holder RiskThe manufacturer chooses an unsuitable Peru Registration Holder or underestimates the importance of local registration control.
Certificate RiskCFS, CFG, ISO 13485 or reference-market evidence are incomplete, expired or inconsistent.
Dossier RiskThe technical file, risk analysis, clinical evidence, labeling or IFU are incomplete or poorly aligned with DIGEMID expectations.
Process RiskVUCE or SUCE filing mechanics are mishandled and delay submission or review.
Lifecycle RiskRenewals, changes, tecnovigilance or holder continuity are weaker than the Peruvian route requires.
